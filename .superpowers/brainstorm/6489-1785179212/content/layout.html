Layout: where do the numbers live while you fiddle?

You tweak pension %, sacrifice and tax codes repeatedly and watch numbers move. The single biggest question is what stays on screen while you do that. Click the one that feels right.

A

Sticky vitals bar + tabbed detail

A slim always-on bar pinned to the top carries the three numbers you actually watch. Everything else lives under tabs, so the page stops being a nine-card scroll.

Trade-off: least disruptive to what exists — cards get regrouped into tabs. But tabs hide things, and comparing bands against NI means clicking back and forth.

B

Cockpit — fixed control panel, live canvas

Left rail is a compact, non-scrolling control panel. The right side is one continuous live readout dominated by a single waterfall showing gross collapsing into net. Detail expands inline rather than living in separate cards.

Trade-off: best for fiddling — move a slider, the waterfall animates, nothing moves out of view. Costs the most rework, and the fixed rail needs a real mobile answer (bottom sheet).

C

Compare-first — two columns are two scenarios

Reframes the whole app around what you said you do: comparing. The screen is permanently split into Current and What-if, each fully editable, with the delta down the middle. Scenario comparison stops being a bolt-on feature and becomes the interface.

Trade-off: the most distinctive and the most opinionated. Brilliant when comparing, but halves the width for the common case where you just want one number — and mobile can only ever show one side at a time.

My recommendation: B, with C's delta idea folded in — the what-if column becomes a toggle on the cockpit rather than the permanent frame. That gets you live-feedback fiddling without paying C's width and mobile costs.
